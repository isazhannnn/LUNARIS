PROJECT NAME

HIGH-LEVEL PROJECT SUMMARY
LUNARIS - The Last Signal is an educational browser game by Parsec Studio, the team of Meiirzhan Issa and Zhasulan Zholdas. Our original concept connects a stranded lunar engineer's story with practical science missions. Players restore a damaged outpost, recover water, build protection, re-establish communication and explore for ice. Four controllable expeditions let learners carry materials, operate equipment, observe consequences and explain their decisions. Selected mission conditions vary between launches, supporting repeated practice. NASA resources inform the scientific explanations, while PIX provides contextual guidance. English and Russian interfaces, keyboard and touch controls, and an offline edition make the game easy to try. Our goal is to help school students practise scientific reasoning and engineering trade-offs through actions they can see and test.

DETAILED PROJECT DESCRIPTION
LUNARIS - The Last Signal was conceived and created by Parsec Studio, the team of Meiirzhan Issa and Zhasulan Zholdas. Our original contribution to the Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer challenge is a story-led learning experience in which science decisions directly affect a damaged lunar outpost. We defined the core concept, educational goals, story requirements and gameplay direction, and made the final creative decisions.

The player controls Alex, a junior engineer left at the station with the helper robot PIX after the crew evacuates. Restoring power, air, water, food, protection and contact with Earth requires balancing limited resources. The story gives the scientific tasks a shared purpose: keep the outpost working and find a way forward.

Four controllable expeditions turn scientific principles into physical actions. In Water Recovery, players carry a sample through collection, filtration, purification, testing and storage, then balance two valves within a flow budget. In Storm Shelter, they carry two materials, compare mass and protection constraints, and position panels over exposed sections. In Earth Link, they calculate round-trip signal time, tune frequency and phase, and wait for a reply. In Ice Crater, they prepare and drive a rover, avoid obstacles, drill without overheating, and save enough energy to return with the sample.

The learning loop is action, observation, explanation and a short knowledge check. PIX offers contextual guidance. Selected layouts, resource constraints, communication distances, sample positions and questions vary between launches. Badges and progress are saved in the browser. English and Russian interfaces, keyboard movement and touch controls support different devices and learners.

NASA resources inform the explanations of life support, growing plants, radiation protection, light-travel time, lunar ice and a historical DONKI example. Nine resources are linked and mapped to their use in the repository. These are educational references, not live telemetry. The numerical game models are simplified teaching tools, not engineering procedures or space-weather predictions.

The game uses HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Canvas 2D, Web Audio and browser local storage. It runs without registration, an API key, a database or an external AI model. An offline HTML edition is included. Automated checks exercise mission completion, resource limits, signal timing and generated scenario configurations. Parsec Studio directed the project and selected the final materials; ChatGPT assisted parts of development and documentation, and image-generation tools produced visibly identified fictional artwork. The original project concept and creative direction belong to our team.

LUNARIS deserves consideration because it turns scientific ideas into decisions players can make and test. Across four controllable missions, learners handle water recovery, protection, communication delay and rover energy, then connect their actions to explanations and questions. Variable mission conditions support repeated practice. Browser access, English and Russian, keyboard and touch controls, and an offline edition reduce barriers to trying the game. Parsec Studio combines this playable experience with traceable NASA resources, public source code and automated checks of mission mechanics.

The intended benefit is approachable practice in scientific reasoning, planning and trade-offs for school students. Classroom learning outcomes have not yet been formally evaluated. The public release provides a working game, source code, a seven-slide presentation, scientific references and reproducible checks.
